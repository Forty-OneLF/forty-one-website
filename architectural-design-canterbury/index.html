Local knowledge

Planning in Canterbury: what to know

Heritage and conservation

The cathedral, St Augustine's Abbey and St Martin's Church form a World Heritage Site, and the city centre and many suburbs are conservation areas. Listed buildings need listed building consent as well as planning. We prepare heritage statements and detail extensions to suit.

Houses in multiple occupation

Parts of Canterbury are covered by an Article 4 direction, so turning a family home into a small HMO needs planning permission there. The council looks at how many HMOs are already nearby, and we check this before you buy or convert.

Water quality rules for new homes

New homes draining to the Canterbury, Westbere or Chartham treatment works must show they won't add nutrients to the Stodmarsh nature reserve. Credits to offset this are now on sale locally, and we build this into the application.

Local policies we design to

Applications are decided under the Canterbury District Local Plan (adopted 2017). A replacement plan has been through its final public consultation but is not yet adopted, so the 2017 policies still apply.

  • DBE3 Principles of DesignThe main design test for all new building, including new homes on garden or infill plots.
  • DBE6 Alterations and ExtensionsExtensions must suit the original house and street and avoid harming neighbours' light, outlook and privacy.
  • DBE4 Residential Space StandardsNew homes and flats must meet national minimum room and floor-area sizes.
  • HD6 Housing in Multiple OccupationControls new HMOs, alongside the Article 4 direction area mapped in the plan.
  • HE6 and HE8 Conservation AreasDevelopment in or near a conservation area must preserve or enhance its character.
  • HE4 and HE5 Listed BuildingsChanges to listed buildings and their settings need to protect their special interest.

FAQs

Common questions in Canterbury

Do I need planning permission in a Canterbury conservation area?

Usually more often than elsewhere. Conservation areas limit permitted development, particularly for roof alterations, side extensions and cladding. We'll tell you what applies to your street on the free call.

Can you help convert a house to an HMO in Canterbury?

Yes. We check whether the property is inside the Article 4 area and how many HMOs are nearby, then prepare the change of use application and layout drawings to meet room size and licensing standards.

My application in Canterbury was refused. What now?

We'll read the decision notice and officer's report, then advise whether a revised application or an appeal is the better route. A well-targeted resubmission is often quicker than an appeal.
