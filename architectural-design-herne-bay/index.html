Local knowledge

Planning in Herne Bay: what to know

Building on a garden plot

Herne Bay has plenty of large gardens, and new homes behind or beside existing houses are common. Officers look closely at access, neighbours' privacy, the street scene and garden sizes. We test these early with a site layout before anything is drawn in detail.

Seafront and town centre

The town centre and seafront are covered by a conservation area, and properties close to the sea can sit in higher flood risk zones. Both affect what you can build and what the application needs to include.

Water quality rules for new homes

Some new homes in Herne Bay connect to a treatment works covered by the Stodmarsh water quality rules. For Herne Bay the requirement is very small and mitigation credits are available, but it still has to be dealt with in the application.

FAQs

Common questions in Herne Bay

Can I build a house in my garden in Herne Bay?

Often, yes, if the plot is big enough and has decent access. We'll look at the site on a free call, then test a layout against the council's usual expectations before you commit to full drawings.

How long does planning take in Herne Bay?

Canterbury City Council aims to decide householder applications within eight weeks of validation and new dwellings within eight to thirteen weeks. Allow a couple of weeks before that for the survey and drawings.

Do you do building regulations drawings too?

Yes. Once planning is approved we produce the technical drawings your builder and building control need, coordinated with a structural engineer where needed.
