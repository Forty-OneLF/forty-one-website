Local knowledge

Planning in Whitstable: what to know

Conservation areas

Much of the old town, the harbour and parts of the seafront sit in conservation areas. Permitted development rights are narrower there, so a rear extension or dormer that would be fine elsewhere may need full planning permission. We check this on the first call.

Coastal flood risk

Properties near the harbour and the seafront can fall within higher flood risk zones. Extensions and new homes there may need a flood risk assessment and raised floor levels, which we design in from the start rather than adding later.

New homes near the coast

New homes in the district usually pay a small per-bedroom contribution towards protecting the coastal bird habitats. It's set by the council and we include it in the application so it doesn't hold up validation.

FAQs

Common questions in Whitstable

Do I need planning permission for an extension in Whitstable?

Not always. Many single-storey rear extensions and loft conversions can go ahead under permitted development, but not in a conservation area, on flats or where earlier permissions removed those rights. We'll tell you which route applies, and a Lawful Development Certificate can confirm it formally.

Which council handles applications in Whitstable?

Canterbury City Council decides planning applications for Whitstable, Tankerton, Seasalter, Chestfield and Swalecliffe. We submit through the Planning Portal and deal with the case officer for you.

Can you visit the property?

Yes. We're based in Whitstable, so a measured survey is usually arranged within days. We survey with a 3D scanner, which captures the whole building accurately in one visit.
