The 2026 changes

What changed on 27 August 2026

New regulations (SI 2026/896) rewrote the permitted development rules for solar on homes. Houses and blocks of flats now have separate rules. In plain English, for a house:

01 · ROOFS

Roof limits are unchanged

On a pitched roof, panels can stand no more than 0.2m off the slope and can't rise above the ridge. On a flat roof the limit is 0.6m, and in a conservation area flat-roof panels need prior approval.

02 · WALLS AND BALCONIES

More room on walls

Wall panels can now project up to 0.4m, or 0.2m where the wall is next to a road. Balconies and roof parapets are covered for the first time.

03 · GARDENS

New height rules in the garden

Stand-alone panels now follow a height table based on how close they are to a boundary, and some positions need the council's prior approval.

04 · LISTED HOMES

Tighter for listed buildings

Permitted development no longer covers panels on a listed house itself, as well as buildings in its grounds. Listed building consent is a separate matter.

If the changes took away something you could have done before, you can still carry it out until the end of 26 August 2027. Planning information last reviewed November 2026.

Conservation areas

Where it gets more careful

You can still fit solar in a conservation area, but which way each panel faces now matters. An Article 4 Direction can remove the rights altogether, so we check every address first.

Key
A

Pitched roof panels: permittedFront and rear slopes, even facing the road, as long as they stay within the roof limits.

B

Walls facing the road: not permittedThe same goes for balconies and parapets. These need a planning application.

C

Flat roofs and front gardens: prior approvalPanels on a flat roof, and garden panels closer to the road than the house (up to 2m high), need the council's prior approval on their appearance. It has 56 days to decide.

FAQs

Common questions about solar planning

Do I need planning permission for solar panels?

Usually not. Most roof-mounted panels on a house are permitted development. It depends on where the panels go, whether you're in a conservation area or protected landscape, and whether the house is listed.

Do solar panels on a flat roof need prior approval?

In a conservation area, National Landscape, National Park, the Broads or a World Heritage Site, yes. The council must approve how panels on a flat roof will look before work starts, and has 56 days to decide.

Is a Lawful Development Certificate worth it?

It's optional, but it confirms in writing that no permission was needed. Buyers' solicitors often ask for one.

What about plug-in solar?

Plug-in panels follow the same planning rules as other domestic solar, with one extra limit: they can't be fixed to timber walls, cladding, balconies or wooden fences.
