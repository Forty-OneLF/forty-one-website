Local knowledge

Planning in Broadstairs: what to know

Neighbourhood plan

Broadstairs and St Peter's has its own neighbourhood plan, which carries real weight in planning decisions alongside Thanet's Local Plan. It sets expectations on design and character, and we reference it directly in applications here.

Conservation areas

The town centre and seafront, St Peter's village, Reading Street and Kingsgate are all conservation areas. Permitted development is narrower there, particularly for roof and street-facing changes.

Coastal and HMO rules

New homes pay a small per-bedroom contribution towards protecting the Thanet Coast, and an Article 4 direction across Thanet means converting a house to a small HMO needs planning permission.

FAQs

Common questions in Broadstairs

Do I need planning permission for an extension in Broadstairs?

Many single-storey rear extensions are permitted development, but not in a conservation area in every case, and not where earlier permissions removed those rights. We'll tell you which route applies on the free call.

Does the neighbourhood plan affect my application?

Yes. The council must take it into account, so applications in Broadstairs and St Peter's are stronger when the design and statement show how they meet its policies.

Can you visit the property?

Yes. We're 20 minutes away in Whitstable and survey with a 3D scanner, so one visit captures everything we need.
